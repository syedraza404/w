Syed Raza

Visiting Assistant Professor of Sociology, Kenyon College

I study how religion and moral judgment shape economic life.

My research follows questions about faith and finance into everyday decisions about investing and borrowing. In Chicago, I examine how Muslims weigh religious guidance, financial expertise, and obligations to their communities when deciding which practices they can participate in.

Research

Divine Dollars

Classificatory Struggles of Islamic Finance in Chicago

My dissertation draws on fourteen months of fieldwork to examine what makes a financial practice religiously permissible in the eyes of Chicago-area Muslims. In public-equity investing, I ask how people come to participate in the same market while continuing to disagree about screening standards and whose guidance they trust.

Contemporary Islam · 2026

Halal enough? Authority, authenticity, and accountability in American Islamic finance

Religion and emerging technologies

I am developing research on how Muslims interpret and debate artificial intelligence and questions in bioethics. I am interested in how these questions enter communal life and who is recognized as qualified to guide the decisions they raise.
